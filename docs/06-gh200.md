# NVIDIA GH200: the same engine on a datacentre GPU

On a rented GH200 (Hopper, sm_90), fastconformer-trt runs parakeet-ultra at **22,776x real time**, 5.9x stock NeMo
on the same GPU, at the same accuracy (test-clean 1.820% vs 1.801%, Earnings-22 10.04% vs 10.03%). Getting there
needed a different mix from the DGX Spark: on Hopper, TensorRT's own FP8 GEMMs beat our Spark-tuned CUTLASS kernels,
while the fused attention and fused subsampling plugins remain essential.

## Setup

- One NVIDIA GH200 480GB on Lambda (us-east-3): Hopper GPU with 97,871 MiB HBM3, 900 W power limit, max SM clock
  1,980 MHz, driver 570.148.08. The NeMo 25.11 container (CUDA 13) ran on this driver through CUDA forward
  compatibility.
- Same repository, image and runner as on the Spark; plugins built with `FC_SM=90`; the Triton attention kernel
  recompiled for the GPU (`relpos_cubin.py`).
- Data: LibriSpeech test-clean (2,620 utterances) and the full Open ASR Leaderboard Earnings-22 test set (2,741).
- FP8 calibration on English only (`LEAN_CALIB` unset; FLEURS was not copied to the box). On the Spark, multilingual
  calibration made no measurable difference.
- Default clocks, no heat soak and no energy measurement (no wall meter in a cloud machine), five timed passes.
  Treat the speeds as throughput at the GPU's default settings, not as a controlled stock-machine comparison like the
  Spark headline.

### The Hopper configuration (T4)

```bash
ASR_MODEL=/data/ultra.nemo MAXB=256 FFN_SKIP=1 LEAN_PREMASK_ONCE=1 LEAN_DWSHIFT=1 LEAN_RELSHIFT=1 \
  LEAN_QKV=1 LEAN_ATTN_PLUGIN=1 LEAN_HB=1 LEAN_POSCACHE=1 ./build_variant.sh ultra_hopper
# run with --frame-budget 19200 --max-batch 128 --dec-batch 256
```

## Why the mix differs from the Spark

Unit tests of the plugins on the GH200 (6,400 x 1,024 inputs, the encoder's shapes):

| Kernel | Ours | cuBLAS |
|---|---|---|
| Feed-forward block (two FP8 GEMMs, SiLU + quantise fused) | 0.279 ms | GEMM1 0.050 ms + GEMM2 0.047 ms |
| QKV projection, heads-first output | 0.171 ms | GEMM 0.040 ms, GEMM + permute 0.109 ms |
| pw1 + GLU | 0.073 ms | GEMM + GLU 0.078 ms |

Our CUTLASS kernels use tile shapes and single-CTA clusters chosen for the Spark's GB10, where they run at 120 to 150
TFLOP/s against a power-limited ceiling of about 146. On Hopper, with roughly ten times the FP8 throughput, those
choices leave most of the tensor cores idle and cuBLAS (through TensorRT) is several times faster. Retuning the CUTLASS
kernels for sm_90 (larger tiles, 2-CTA clusters, more pipeline stages) would probably recover the fused epilogues'
advantage. That retune was later done on an H100 ([docs/08-h100.md](08-h100.md)): Hopper tiles, 2-CTA clusters and
FP8 fast accumulation made our feed-forward block 1.8x faster, but TensorRT's GEMMs still give the faster engine.

What stays essential on Hopper:

- **RelPosAttn**: fused relative-position attention, now fed either by our QKVHeads plugin or by TensorRT's own GEMM.
- **SubConv02**: without a subsampling plugin TensorRT's handling of the masked subsampling convolutions costs
  376 ms per batch (T5). The tensor-core conv.3 fusion (`SUB_PW3`) that helps on the Spark is slightly slower here
  (14.4 vs 13.7 ms).
- **Frame-budget batching** (+20% over plain batch 32).

## Limits seen

- At 22,776x the pipeline spends about 0.62 s in the encoder, 0.38 s in the decoder and 0.09 s in preprocessing per
  test-clean pass, with the decoder overlapping the encoder. The decoder is now about a third of the work.
- Encoder batches of 256 utterances fail with decoder groups of 256 (an assertion in the compiled feature extractor,
  also seen on the Spark with groups smaller than the encoder batch); use decoder groups larger than the encoder batch.
